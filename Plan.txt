# ClosetIQ — Complete Product Requirements and Development Plan

**Version:** 3.0  
**Status:** Draft for review  
**Product type:** Personal wardrobe management and intelligent clothing planner  
**Architecture:** Cloud-based web application  
**Cost objective:** Free-tier hosting, database, weather services, and AI APIs, with no paid services required for core functionality.

---

# 1. Product Vision

ClosetIQ is a personal wardrobe intelligence platform that manages clothing throughout its entire lifecycle: adding new garments, organizing outfits, wearing clothes, managing laundry baskets, washing, drying, ironing, and returning garments to use.

The application combines:

- A digital wardrobe with photographs and custom labels.
- Manually created clothing pairs.
- AI-assisted outfit recommendations.
- Natural-language lifestyle and clothing requirements.
- Weather-aware outfit suggestions.
- Calendar-aware planning.
- Laundry baskets with scheduling and progress tracking.
- Garment-level washing, drying, and ironing states.
- Weekly and long-term clothing schedules.

The goal is to generate a realistic, continuously updated clothing plan that respects the garments the user actually owns and their real-world availability.

## Core principles

1. **Availability first:** Never recommend a garment that is unavailable.
2. **User control:** Every AI-generated suggestion can be reviewed or overridden.
3. **Routine-aware:** Understand the user's recurring clothing requirements.
4. **Calendar-aware:** Adapt to holidays, parties, workdays, and schedule changes.
5. **Laundry-aware:** Understand which clothes are waiting, being washed, drying, or awaiting ironing.
6. **Cloud-based:** Store wardrobe data and photographs in hosted services.
7. **Free-first:** Avoid paid APIs and infrastructure for core functionality.
8. **Graceful degradation:** Essential wardrobe, laundry, and planning features continue working when an AI API reaches its quota.

# 3. Digital Wardrobe Management

## 3.1 Adding garments

Users can add individual garments with:

- Photograph.
- Name.
- Category and subcategory.
- Color and pattern.
- Custom labels.
- Occasion and formality.
- Season and weather suitability.
- Fabric and comfort details, where known.
- Laundry and ironing requirements.
- Purchase date and price, optionally.
- Notes and personal preferences.

Examples of categories include shirts, T-shirts, trousers, jeans, vests, underwear, socks, shoes, jackets, nightwear, and accessories.

## 3.2 AI-assisted classification

When a photograph is uploaded, AI can suggest the garment's category, color, pattern, occasion labels, formality, and weather suitability.

The user must be able to review and correct these suggestions before saving.

Manual entry must always be supported.

## 3.3 Custom labels

Users can create labels such as Office, College, Party, Casual, Nightwear, Travel, Gym, Rainy Weather, and Favorite.

A garment may have multiple labels. Labels are used for filtering, outfit generation, and matching lifestyle requirements.

## 3.4 Adding and removing garments

Users can add new clothing whenever they want, edit existing garments, replace photographs, archive garments, or mark them as no longer owned.

Archived garments must not appear in future recommendations, but historical wear records should remain available.

# 4. Garment Lifecycle and Availability

Every garment must have a clearly defined current state.

## 4.1 Garment states

The proposed states are:

1. **Ready to Wear:** Clean, available, and prepared for use.
2. **Worn — Reusable:** Worn but still eligible for reuse under its configured rules.
3. **Needs Laundry:** Requires washing before another use.
4. **In Laundry Basket:** Assigned to a laundry basket and waiting for processing or collection.
5. **In Laundry:** Its laundry basket is being processed.
6. **Drying:** Washed but not yet ready for use.
7. **Needs Ironing:** Clean but requires ironing before it can be considered ready.
8. **Reserved:** Allocated to an upcoming event or protected outfit.
9. **Unavailable:** Temporarily inaccessible, damaged, borrowed, or otherwise unusable.

The user can manually update a garment's state.

### Important distinction

A garment can be clean but still unavailable because it is drying or needs ironing. Similarly, a garment in a pending laundry basket must not appear as an available outfit choice.

The application must distinguish physical availability, cleanliness, and preparation requirements.

## 4.2 Wear history

For every garment, track:

- Total number of wears.
- Number of wears since the last wash.
- Last worn date.
- Last washed date.
- Current state.
- Upcoming scheduled uses.
- Current laundry basket, if assigned.
- Whether ironing is required.

When the user marks an outfit as worn, the application updates the history of all garments in that outfit.

Washing and reuse rules can differ by garment category. For example, socks and underwear generally require washing after use, while certain trousers and jackets may be reused.

## 4.3 State transitions

A typical lifecycle is:

Ready to Wear → Worn → Reusable or Needs Laundry → In Laundry Basket → In Laundry → Drying → Needs Ironing or Ready to Wear.

The exact transition depends on the garment's washing and ironing requirements.

A user must be able to override the state when the real-world situation differs from the expected lifecycle.

# 5. Laundry Baskets — New Core Feature

Laundry baskets are independent entities that organize garments into batches for planned laundry activities.

A basket is more than a collection of garment IDs: it has its own schedule, status, contents, and completion history.

## 5.1 Creating a basket

Users can create any number of laundry baskets.

Each basket contains:

- Basket name.
- Optional description.
- Selected garments.
- Scheduled laundry date.
- Current basket status.
- Optional estimated completion date and time.
- Optional laundry notes.
- Creation date.
- Start date.
- Completion date.
- Garments successfully processed.

Example basket names:

- Monday Laundry.
- Whites and Light Colors.
- Weekend Laundry.
- Office Clothes.
- Urgent Laundry.

Users should be free to organize baskets however they prefer.

## 5.2 Adding clothes to a basket

When a garment is added to a basket, its state must immediately change to **In Laundry Basket**.

This transition must take effect as soon as the operation is successfully saved to the backend.

Consequences:

- The garment becomes unavailable for new outfit recommendations.
- Future plans using the garment must be checked.
- The weekly planner recalculates affected dates when appropriate.
- The garment appears in the basket's contents.
- The application records the state change in its history.

If the garment is already assigned to another active laundry basket, the application must prevent duplicate assignment unless the user first removes it from the existing basket.

The application should warn users when adding a garment that is reserved for an upcoming event.

## 5.3 Removing clothes from a basket

Users must be able to remove a garment from a basket that has not yet been processed.

When a garment is removed, the application should restore its previous valid state, unless the user chooses a different state.

For example:

- A garment that was ready to wear returns to Ready to Wear.
- A garment that already needed washing returns to Needs Laundry.
- A garment reserved for an event may return to its appropriate reserved state if the reservation is still valid.

The application must preserve state history rather than simply assuming every removed garment is clean.

Removing garments from a basket that is already in progress requires confirmation, because the laundry process may already have started.

## 5.4 Basket statuses

Each basket has one of three primary statuses:

**Pending**
- The basket has been created.
- It may have a scheduled date.
- Its contents are waiting to be processed.
- All assigned garments remain in the In Laundry Basket state.

**In Progress**
- Laundry processing has started.
- The basket records its start date and time.
- Its garments are unavailable for use.
- The user can review the basket contents and update processing details.

**Completed**
- The user confirms that the laundry batch has been completed.
- The completion date and time are recorded.
- Garment wear counts since the last wash are reset.
- The last washed date is updated.
- Each garment moves to its appropriate post-laundry state.
- The basket's completion history is retained.

A completed basket should not automatically be reopened when a new basket is created.

## 5.5 Scheduling a basket

Users can assign a scheduled date and optionally an expected completion time.

The application can then:

- Show upcoming laundry batches on a calendar.
- Remind the user when a basket is due.
- Warn if the basket contains garments needed for upcoming outfits.
- Estimate which garments will remain available during the laundry period.
- Update weekly plans based on the expected processing schedule.

The scheduled date is a plan, not proof that laundry has started. The basket changes to In Progress only when the user starts it or explicitly authorizes a configured automatic transition.

## 5.6 Basket completion and garment refresh

When the user marks a basket as completed, the application must refresh the garments included in that batch.

For each successfully processed garment:

1. Reset the wear count since the last wash to zero.
2. Update the last washed date.
3. Record the completed laundry batch.
4. Remove the active basket assignment.
5. Recalculate the garment's post-laundry state.
6. Revalidate any future outfit reservations and schedules.

The post-laundry state depends on preparation requirements:

- If the garment is dry and needs no ironing, mark it Ready to Wear.
- If it is still drying, mark it Drying.
- If it needs ironing, mark it Needs Ironing.
- If the garment is damaged or otherwise unavailable, preserve the appropriate unavailable state.

**Important:** Completing a basket must not automatically make every garment Ready to Wear if it still needs drying or ironing.

The user can confirm the final condition of the batch or update individual garments after completion.

## 5.7 Basket dashboard

The laundry section should show:

- Pending baskets.
- Baskets due today.
- In-progress baskets.
- Completed baskets.
- Scheduled dates.
- Expected completion times.
- Number of garments per basket.
- Garments awaiting ironing.
- Laundry-related scheduling warnings.

Users should be able to filter and sort baskets by date, status, and name.

## 5.8 Laundry reminders

Examples:

- "Your Monday Laundry basket is due today."
- "Three office shirts in your basket are required later this week."
- "Your laundry is still marked In Progress."
- "Two washed garments still need ironing."

Reminders must be configurable to avoid unnecessary notifications.

# 6. Ironing Management

Ironing is a separate preparation stage in the clothing lifecycle.

## 6.1 Garment-level ironing requirement

Each garment can have an ironing policy:

- Ironing not required.
- Ironing required after washing.
- Ironing optional.
- User decides after washing.

The policy should be configurable per garment or category.

## 6.2 Needs Ironing state

A garment enters Needs Ironing when it has been washed and is dry but requires ironing before it should be worn.

Such a garment must be excluded from immediately wearable outfit recommendations.

The application may show it as a possible future outfit if ironing can be completed before the planned use.

## 6.3 Ironing actions

Users should be able to:

- Mark a garment as needing ironing.
- Mark it as ironed.
- Update its state to Ready to Wear after ironing.
- Select several garments and mark them ironed together.
- View a list of garments awaiting ironing.
- See when an upcoming outfit depends on a garment needing ironing.

Completing ironing must not reset the wear count or change the last washed date. Only a washing operation resets the wear count since the last wash.

## 6.4 Ironing reminders

Examples:

- "Your formal shirt for tomorrow still needs ironing."
- "Four clean garments are waiting to be ironed."
- "Your planned office outfit is unavailable until ironing is completed."

Ironing reminders should use the calendar and weekly plan to prioritize garments needed soon.

# 7. Manual Pairs and Outfit Management

## 7.1 Manually created pairs

Users can manually select a specific shirt and trousers, name the combination, and save it as a preferred pair.

A pair can include shoes, jackets, and accessories.

Each pair supports:

- Name and description.
- Selected garments.
- Labels and occasion.
- Favorite status.
- Notes.
- Editing, duplication, and deletion.

Manually created pairs must be preserved as defined by the user.

## 7.2 AI-generated outfits

AI or a recommendation engine can generate combinations using the user's wardrobe.

Candidate outfits are ranked by:

- Color compatibility.
- Formality and occasion.
- Weather suitability.
- Personal preferences.
- Previous ratings.
- Recent repetition.
- Current availability.

AI-generated outfits remain separate from manually saved pairs. Users can save generated outfits as new pairs.

## 7.3 Availability validation

An outfit is immediately wearable only when every required garment is available and properly prepared.

An outfit must be rejected as immediately wearable if any required garment is:

- In a laundry basket.
- In laundry.
- Drying.
- Needs laundry.
- Needs ironing.
- Otherwise unavailable.

A garment reserved for another incompatible event must also be excluded.

The application may suggest the outfit as a future possibility if the relevant garment can become ready in time.

# 8. Lifestyle Rules and Natural-Language Planning

Users should be able to explain their clothing requirements in natural language.

Example:

"Monday to Friday, I go to the office. Every day I need one formal shirt, one T-shirt, one vest, one pair of underwear, and one pair of socks. I use the same office shoes throughout the month. Every night from Sunday to Saturday, I wear a nightwear pair."

The AI converts the description into structured rules for review.

## 8.1 Routine structure

Each routine contains:

- Routine name.
- Applicable weekdays.
- Start and end dates.
- Required categories and quantities.
- Occasion and labels.
- Garment reuse duration.
- Washing and ironing requirements.
- Mandatory or flexible status.
- Optional consecutive-day reuse restrictions.

## 8.2 Interpretation workflow

1. The user describes a routine.
2. AI extracts days, categories, quantities, and usage rules.
3. The application presents the interpreted rules.
4. The user edits or confirms them.
5. The planning engine checks wardrobe feasibility.
6. The system generates a schedule.
7. The user can change the rules and regenerate affected dates.

If the description is ambiguous, the application asks for clarification.

## 8.3 Laundry-aware routines

The planning engine must consider whether routine requirements can be satisfied while garments are unavailable for washing, drying, or ironing.

For example, if the user needs a clean formal shirt every weekday, the planner must ensure enough shirts are available or schedule laundry early enough to return garments to use.

Shoe reuse, nightwear, underwear, and socks must each follow their configured usage rules.

---

# 9. Weather and Personal Comfort

The application uses current and forecast conditions where available:

- Temperature.
- Feels-like temperature.
- Rain probability.
- Humidity.
- Wind.
- Expected conditions during outdoor activities.

Users can manually report today's conditions as hot, cold, rainy, humid, windy, or comfortable.

Manual reports should influence recommendations without overwriting external forecast data.

The system can learn personal preferences from explicit feedback, such as preferring lighter clothing in hot weather.

Weather data must not override clothing availability or mandatory event requirements.

---

# 10. Calendar and Events

Users can create events such as office days, college, holidays, parties, interviews, weddings, travel, gym sessions, and custom occasions.

Each event may include:

- Date and time.
- Dress code.
- Labels.
- Notes.
- Required garments.
- Optional reserved outfit.

## 10.1 Automatic replanning

When Wednesday becomes a holiday, the planner removes the normal office requirements for that date.

When a party is added, the planner suggests suitable clothing and checks garment availability.

When an event is cancelled or moved, its reservations are released and the plan is recalculated.

## 10.2 Conflict handling

The application detects:

- Garments reserved for incompatible events.
- Garments in laundry that are required soon.
- Clean garments that still need ironing.
- Insufficient inventory after calendar changes.
- Laundry completion dates that conflict with important events.

The user can lock an outfit, replace a suggestion, regenerate selected dates, or approve proposed changes.

# 11. Laundry-Aware Weekly and Long-Term Planning

The planner generates realistic clothing schedules for the next seven days or a custom period.

It considers:

- Lifestyle rules.
- Calendar events.
- Weather.
- Manual pairs.
- Generated outfits.
- Garment availability.
- Active laundry baskets.
- Scheduled laundry dates.
- Expected completion dates.
- Drying and ironing requirements.
- Reuse and washing rules.
- Personal preferences.
- Outfit repetition.

## 11.1 Laundry simulation

Before starting a basket, the user can simulate its effect on future plans.

The application reports:

- Which garments will become unavailable.
- Which outfits remain possible.
- Whether the upcoming week can be covered.
- Which categories are becoming scarce.
- Which events may be affected.
- Whether drying or ironing is likely to create a shortage.

## 11.2 Feasibility reporting

Examples:

- "Five clean formal shirts are required, but only three are available."
- "Your planned Friday shirt is in the Monday Laundry basket."
- "Your party outfit requires a shirt that still needs ironing."
- "Your current socks inventory is insufficient for the selected period."

The system must propose feasible alternatives rather than inventing garment availability.

## 11.3 Partial replanning

When a basket is created, its scheduled date changes, or it is completed, the planner should identify affected dates.

Locked outfits and unrelated user decisions should be preserved unless the user approves changes.

# 12. Additional Features

## Wardrobe analytics

- Total and available garments.
- Most and least used items.
- Wear frequency.
- Outfit repetition.
- Category shortages.
- Laundry frequency.
- Number of garments awaiting ironing.

Provide configurable reminders for laundry baskets, drying, ironing, upcoming events, and planned outfits.

# 13. Cloud Architecture

## 13.1 Data flow

1. The React frontend sends authenticated requests to FastAPI.
2. The backend validates ownership and business rules.
3. Supabase PostgreSQL stores the structured data.
4. Supabase Storage stores clothing photographs.
5. AI requests are sent through a backend-only provider integration.
6. Weather information is retrieved from the selected weather service.
7. The planning engine evaluates routines, calendar events, garment availability, baskets, and preparation states.
8. The frontend displays updated recommendations and schedules.

## 13.2 Database entities

The database should include:

- Users and preferences.
- Garments.
- Garment images.
- Categories and labels.
- Wear history and lifecycle events.
- Laundry baskets.
- Laundry basket garment assignments.
- Laundry batch history.
- Manual pairs.
- Outfits.
- Lifestyle rules.
- Calendar events.
- Planned outfits and garment allocations.
- Notifications.
- AI interpretation records where necessary.

### Laundry basket data

Each basket should include:

- Basket ID.
- User ID.
- Name and description.
- Scheduled date and time.
- Expected completion date and time.
- Status: Pending, In Progress, or Completed.
- Created timestamp.
- Started timestamp.
- Completed timestamp.
- Optional notes.

Each basket-to-garment assignment should record the garment, basket, assignment time, previous state, processing outcome, and relevant history.

A garment must not belong to two active laundry baskets simultaneously.

## 13.3 Data consistency

Adding a garment to a basket must update its availability and basket assignment as one logical operation.

Completing a basket must update all affected garments, reset their wear counts since washing, record washing dates, and update the basket status consistently.

The backend should use database transactions where appropriate so partial failures do not leave garments and baskets in contradictory states.

## 13.4 Security and privacy

- Keep credentials on the backend.
- Use authentication and row-level access policies.
- Restrict access to each user's garments and images.
- Avoid sending unnecessary personal information to AI services.
- Disclose external data processing.
- Support data export and deletion.

# 14. AI and Deterministic Logic

AI handles tasks that benefit from language or image understanding:

- Clothing classification.
- Natural-language lifestyle interpretation.
- Styling suggestions.
- Recommendation explanations.
- Conversational plan changes.

Deterministic backend logic handles:

- Availability.
- Wear counts.
- Laundry basket status.
- Laundry completion.
- Drying and ironing transitions.
- Calendar conflicts.
- Garment reservations.
- Inventory feasibility.
- Schedule validation.
- Persistence of approved changes.

AI must not independently override availability rules or fabricate garments.

## Free-tier quota handling

When an AI provider reaches its quota:

- Existing wardrobe functionality remains available.
- Confirmed routines can still generate schedules.
- Manual pairs remain usable.
- Laundry baskets can be managed.
- Ironing and wear statuses can be updated.
- The application explains which AI feature is unavailable.
- The user can retry later or use manual alternatives.

There must be no silent fallback to a paid API.

Test lifecycle transitions, basket completion, inventory shortages, calendar conflicts, AI failures, access controls, backups, and mobile usability.

# 16. Acceptance Criteria

The initial release is complete when:

1. Users can add, edit, archive, and manage garments.
2. Users can manually create and modify clothing pairs.
3. Adding a garment to a basket immediately changes its state to In Laundry Basket.
4. A garment cannot belong to two active laundry baskets simultaneously.
5. Baskets support scheduling and Pending, In Progress, and Completed states.
6. Removing an unprocessed garment restores its appropriate previous state.
7. Completing a basket updates washing dates and resets wear counts.
8. Completion does not incorrectly mark drying or ironing garments as Ready to Wear.
9. Users can mark garments as Needs Ironing and update them after ironing.
10. AI-generated outfits exclude unavailable garments.
11. Natural-language routines become editable, structured requirements.
12. The system detects insufficient inventory before confirming an impossible schedule.
13. Weather and calendar changes trigger appropriate recommendations and replanning.
14. Laundry simulation identifies which outfits remain possible.
15. The weekly planner respects basket schedules, drying, ironing, and reuse rules.
16. Locked outfits are preserved during unrelated changes.
17. Cloud data is protected by authentication and appropriate access policies.
18. Core functionality remains available when AI quotas are exhausted.
19. Users can export and restore their wardrobe and laundry data.

# 17. Out of Scope for the Initial Release

The initial version does not require:

- A public community or social network.
- A clothing marketplace.
- Paid AI APIs.
- Paid cloud infrastructure.
- Advanced virtual try-on.
- Automatic physical laundry detection.
- Calendar integrations without explicit authorization.
- Perfect AI fashion judgment.

These can be considered later if they justify their complexity.

---

# 18. Definition of Success

ClosetIQ succeeds when a user can maintain a digital wardrobe, describe their clothing routines, create manual pairs, receive weather-aware recommendations, track laundry baskets, manage ironing, and generate realistic weekly plans that adapt to real-world changes.

Its defining capability is not simply recommending attractive outfits. It is producing a feasible, personalized clothing plan based on the user's actual garments, routine, calendar, and preparation status.

**Recommended implementation order:**

Digital wardrobe → Clothing lifecycle → Manual pairs → Laundry baskets and ironing → Outfit intelligence → Lifestyle rules → Calendar and weather → Laundry-aware scheduling → Advanced features.

This document is the proposed baseline for review before implementation begins.
